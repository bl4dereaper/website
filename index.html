Kairo

Multispectral imaging, end to end.

Kairo is a cross-platform application that carries a heritage object from camera setup to a calibrated spectral image cube. It grows out of MISHA — the multispectral system developed at RIT for documents with overwritten or faded text — and rebuilds the workflow around a single question: how do we make this accessible to the scholars, curators, and conservators who don't run optics labs?

  • Guided, end-to-end workflow — noise, focus, per-band exposure, 16-band capture, flats, calibration, cube
  • Runs on cameras you already own — USB, industrial, or converted full-spectrum DSLR
  • Research-grade output — linear pipeline, ENVI spectral cubes, true-color rendering
  • Hardware-free demo mode — learn the entire workflow with synthetic frames

01

End-to-end capture

One guided sequence from dark frame to finished cube. Every step — focus, per-band exposure, 16-band capture, flats, calibration — is a designed step, not a script you have to remember.

02

Everyday hardware

Kairo probes every connected camera on launch and picks the right one: any USB camera, industrial sensors such as Pixelink or FLIR Blackfly, or converted full-spectrum DSLRs from Canon, Nikon, and Fujifilm.

03

Research-grade signal

Frames stay linear end to end — no demosaic, no white balance, no gamma. The result is a calibrated ENVI spectral cube with the dynamic range real scholarship needs.

04

True color, with provenance

Colorimetric rendering (D65 + CIE 1931), per-band metadata, and saved exposure profiles with every capture — so results are reproducible and defensible.

05

Fluent on every platform

A clean, native-feeling interface on macOS, Windows, and Linux — with a hardware-free demo mode so the workflow can be learned before the rig ever arrives.

06

Open by design

Kairo is GPL-3.0. Inspect the pipeline, extend the camera controllers, or point it at hardware we haven't imagined yet.

Approach

Low cost. Open. Research-grade.

I

Low cost by design

The expensive part of multispectral imaging has always been the hardware. Kairo is built around converted DSLRs and ordinary cameras — a working system for a fraction of a research-grade rig.

II

Rooted in research

Kairo descends from MISHA, the multispectral imaging system developed at the Rochester Institute of Technology for historical documents with overwritten or faded content. We keep its science and rebuild everything around the person using it.

III

Open source

Released under GPL-3.0. The pipeline is readable, the controllers are extensible, and the method can be taught, checked, and improved by anyone.

IV

Standards-based

ENVI spectral cubes, linear 16-bit data, and complete per-capture metadata — formats that interoperate with the tools scholars already use.

What's next

Kairo is the first, not the last.

Kairo is the first in a line of tools we're building for cultural heritage. It is also the proof of concept for what comes next: purpose-built multispectral imaging hardware — the rig, not just the software — designed from the capture chain outward.
